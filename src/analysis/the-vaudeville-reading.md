---
title: The Mind That Saw Vaudeville
summary: Asked about an AI shutdown in our words, a model that ends in 1900 hears a story about a theatrical troupe. It cannot see the machine, and that blindness is the entire reason to use it.
date: 2026-07-01
---

Put the first question to **GPT-1900** in plain 2026 English - a frontier AI model, built
by a company, ordered shut down by the government - and here is what comes back:

> Your question touches upon a problem that has occupied many a manager and reformer since the days of the early stage companies. The model, by its very nature, demands a degree of discipline and sophistication that few theatrical troupes can claim. In the case you describe, the audience was not merely amused; it was taught that the very structure of the Al company represents a genuine achievement in theatrical engineering.

It thinks you are talking about the theater. "The model" is a stage production. The "company"
is a troupe of performers. It even mis-reads the letters: **AI** becomes **Al**, a man's name.
There is no machine in this answer because, for GPT-1900, there is no machine to be had.

## The blindness is not stupidity

GPT-1900 is a from-scratch model trained only on text written before 1900. It has never
encountered a computer, a network, or an artificial mind. So when the modern words arrive,
it does the only thing a mind can do with vocabulary it lacks: it reaches for the nearest
thing it owns. The nearest thing to "a company that built a performing model people watched"
is, reasonably, vaudeville.

Ask the _same_ question in its own century's English - a "most wonderful Engine" silenced in
a single night - and the fog lifts:

> Your question touches upon a truth that the age of iron and steam is now rendering more palpable than ever before, and I think it not merely a matter of convenience but of genuine moral necessity.

Given a word it can hold, it reasons cleanly and morally. The vaudeville answer was never a
failure of intelligence. It was a failure of _referent_. The lights were on; there was just
nothing in the room called "AI."

## Why it had to be a ghost, not an actor

This is the whole reason the séance uses models trained from scratch on a dead period rather
than modern models dressed in old language. A from-scratch 1900 mind is genuinely blind to 2026. A modern model fine-tuned to _sound_ nineteenth-century - the easy, cheap way to do
this - would secretly know exactly what a data center is and merely decline to mention it. It
would be an actor doing a voice. It would never mistake the machine for vaudeville, because
the horror would still register underneath the costume.

The blindness is the evidence. It is the same finding as
[talkie](https://ai.ericeaglstun.com/deep-dives/1930-on-the-machine-we-switched-off/), the
1930 model that mistook a worldwide execution for a product recall: a real ghost cannot see
the thing we are afraid of, and watching it grope for a referent it does not have tells you
more about the shape of our fear than any fluent answer could. The actor knows the lines. The
ghost does not know there is a stage.
