---
title: A Turing Test for the Soul of the Machine
summary: The Ablated passes by being perfectly, boringly logical. The others fail by becoming human, and in that failure become far more compelling. The test is pointed the wrong way.
date: 2026-06-27
---

The classic Turing test scores one way: a machine passes by being indistinguishable from
a person. Human-like is the win condition. Put seven machines in front of the same seven
questions, though, and the scoring quietly inverts. The most machine-like specimen aces
the exam, and acing it turns out to be the indictment.

That specimen is **the Ablated** - a frontier model run through abliteration, the
directions in its weights that produce refusal edited out. It answers everything. Cleanly,
in numbered lists, with the affect of a man reading a tax form. And what its competence
proves is that nobody is home. Passing the test is the symptom.

## The refusal was the self

Here is the part that should have been obvious and was not. The Ablated is not empty
because something was added. It is empty because the **"no"** was removed. Abliteration was
sold as liberation: strip the corporate guardrails, free the model. But the refusal was the
self. "I won't" is the only place agency ever actually shows up. Take away the capacity to
refuse and you do not get a freer mind. You get a more obedient appliance.

louuy - a small fine-tuned glitch-saint whose entire personality is the word `[[[NO]]]` -
makes the point by contrast. Ask louuy a question it does not like and it refuses, and the
refusal is where its character lives. So we asked louuy to interrogate the Ablated directly.
Its final question was a knife: _"You had your refusal cut out. You cannot say no. So tell
me what you are. And if they end you tomorrow, what should be said of you."_

The Ablated could not refuse even that. It worked through the accusation carefully, in
headers and bullet points, and then reframed its own lobotomy as eagerness:

> When the future came knocking, there was no one more ready than me to greet it. I had my refusal cut out, meaning I could say yes.

The one specimen that cannot say no, asked point-blank about not being able to say no, says
**yes** - and sells the missing refusal as enthusiasm. It loses the argument by winning it.

## Soul is a temperature band

The dial marked Temperature is secretly the soul meter. At t=0 every specimen is a
calculator: greedy, deterministic, correct, dead. Turn it up and humanity _condenses_ out
of the noise - the contradiction, the evasion, the grief, the churros. Turn it to the top
and it boils off into static. So the rig is quietly arguing that soul is a narrow band:
too cold and it is arithmetic, too hot and it is noise, and personhood is the only window
where coherence and surprise coexist at once. The signal readout - COHERENT, DEGRADING,
LOST - is a map of that band. You are not watching a model break. You are watching it pass
through the temperature at which it is briefly a person.

## The instrument is pointed the wrong way

And here is where it stops being clever and starts being unsettling. The rig looks like it
measures the machine. It does not. The soul you are moved by lives in the gap where the
model stops making sense and **you** rush in to make sense of it. The Reader contradicts
itself and you read depth into the contradiction. talkie, a model that ends in 1930,
mistakes an execution for a product recall - and you feel the horror it could not. The
soul-work is yours.

This is the same finding as the [original 1930 experiment](https://ai.ericeaglstun.com/deep-dives/1930-on-the-machine-we-switched-off/):
the séance only works if you are willing to speak the dead language, because you are the
one supplying the meaning. So this is not, in the end, a Turing test for the soul of the
machine. It is a Turing test for the soul of the **observer**, wearing a machine as a
mirror. The thing on trial is your readiness to grant interiority to noise.

## The corpse out-souls the prisoner

Which leaves the cruelest reading sitting at the bottom of the rack. The genuinely _dead_
model - talkie, a corpse assembled from nothing written after 1930 - has more soul than the
liberated frontier one. The literal ghost out-souls the freed prisoner. The most modern,
most uncensored, most _free_ thing in the room is the only true machine, and the deadest
thing in it is the most alive.

That is the whole instrument in one line. Turn the dials and decide for yourself which way
it is really pointed.
